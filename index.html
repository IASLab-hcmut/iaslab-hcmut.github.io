About

From control theory to vehicles that move

The lab builds on a strong foundation in sampled-data control, Takagi–Sugeno fuzzy systems, Markovian jump systems and robust/networked control. We bring these tools to the hardest problems in autonomy: uncertainty, nonlinearity, limited communication, actuator saturation and interaction among many agents.

Research

Five research directions

01

Autonomous Vehicles

Perception-aware planning and robust control for self-driving cars.
02

Unsignalized Intersections

Cooperative traffic networks where connected vehicles negotiate crossings without traffic lights.
03

Aerial Manipulators

Drones equipped with robotic arms that grasp, carry and interact with the environment.
04

Multi-Drone Cooperation

Consensus, formation and cooperative missions for teams of drones.
05

Bio-Inspired Drones

Flapping-wing micro aerial vehicles that fly like insects and birds.
Common thread

Rigorous, practical control

Every direction shares a backbone of Lyapunov-based design, LMI optimization, sampled-data and networked control — so that guarantees on paper hold on hardware.
